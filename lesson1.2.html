TikTok & Viral Content

Explore how content becomes viral, understand social media algorithms, and learn to discuss digital trends using comparatives and superlatives.

Reading: How Content Goes Viral

TikTok, trending faster than any other social media app, has changed how we create and share content. Its powerful algorithm decides what becomes viral by showing videos to users who like similar posts. A simple dance or funny challenge can gain millions of views overnight and go viral globally.

Hashtags like #SavageLoveDance or #SilhouetteChallenge unite millions of users. Content creators compete to make the most creative versions, using trending sounds and effects. Viral videos often spark challenges that spread like wildfire – the more engaging the content, the faster it grows.

But not every trending video succeeds. The best ones combine perfect timing, catchy music, and easy-to-copy moves. TikTok's algorithm favors content that keeps users scrolling longer, making some hashtags explode while others fade quickly.

Critical Thinking Questions
1
Why does TikTok's algorithm make some videos viral while others fail, and how does this affect content creators?
2

3

Grammar: Comparatives & Superlatives

Using Comparatives & Superlatives in Digital Context

Rules

• Short adjectives: -er / -est (fast → faster → fastest)

• Long adjectives: more / most (popular → more popular → most popular)

• Exceptions: good → better → best, bad → worse → worst

Comparatives and Superlatives are special forms of adjectives and adverbs that we use when we want to compare things, people, or actions.

Comparative (сравнительная степень)

We use it to compare two things:

• TikTok is more popular among teenagers than Facebook.

• Viral videos spread faster than traditional media.

Superlative (превосходная степень)

We use it to compare two things:

• This is the most viral challenge of the year.

• TikTok has the fastest-growing user base.

Speaking Tips
